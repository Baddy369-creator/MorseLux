SOS in Morse code

SOS in Morse code is :··· ––– ··· three short, three long and three short signals again. Operators send it without gaps between the letters, as one single signal – that is why it is recognised at once.

How to send SOS

  1. Three short. Three short signals: ··· (the S).
  2. Three long. Three signals, each three times as long: ––– (the O).
  3. Three short. ··· again – no pause in between, as one signal.
  4. Pause and repeat. Wait a few seconds and start again until someone answers.
Important: In a real emergency call the emergency number first (911 in the US, 112 in Europe). SOS in Morse is for situations where that is not possible.

SOS and the Titanic

On the night of 14–15 April 1912 the Titanic's radio operators first sent the older distress call CQD and then also the new SOS – one of the first times SOS was used in a famous emergency. The ship Carpathia picked up the calls and rescued the survivors. Afterwards, the rules for ship radio were made much stricter, including a radio watch around the clock.

Where SOS comes from

SOS was agreed as the distress signal at an international radio conference in Berlin in 1906 and used from 1908. Before that, many operators sent “CQD”. The rhythm ··· ––– ··· won because it stands out even in poor reception.

In Morse radio, SOS is one of the procedural signs: signs sent without gaps. You can play it as sound or light in the Morse code translator. In the MorseLux app the SOS feature is always free.

Frequently asked questions

What does SOS look like in Morse code?

··· ––– ··· – three dots, three dashes, three dots. It is often written as ... --- ... but sent without gaps between S, O and S.

What does SOS stand for?

Nothing – SOS is not an abbreviation. It was chosen because the rhythm is easy to send and hard to mistake. “Save Our Souls” and “Save Our Ship” were made up later.

How do I send SOS with a flashlight?

Short flash for a dot (about half a second), three times as long for a dash, with a short dark pause in between. After each SOS wait a few seconds, then repeat.

How do I knock SOS?

Three quick knocks, three slow (or scraping) knocks, three quick knocks – then a pause and again. It works on walls, pipes and doors.

Is SOS still official today?

At sea, the Morse distress call was replaced in 1999 by an automatic radio and satellite system (GMDSS). SOS is still understood worldwide, as light, sound or knocking. In a real emergency always call the emergency number first: 911 in the US, 112 in Europe.
