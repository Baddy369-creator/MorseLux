Prosigns and Q codes in Morse radio

Some signs are sent without a gap, as one signal – the procedural signs (prosigns), for example for “end” or “please wait”. On top come Q codes and short abbreviations. Here are the most important ones, with meaning and sound.

The most important prosigns

SignMorseMeaning
<AR>·–·–·End of message
<SK>···–·–End of contact
<KN>–·––·Go ahead – only the station called
K–·–Go ahead (any station)
<BT>–···–Break, new paragraph (like “=”)
<AS>·–···Please wait
<SN>···–·Understood
<KA>–·–·–Start of message
<HH>········Error – I will repeat
<SOS>···–––···Distress signal (more about SOS)

Our audio decoder recognises these signs and shows them in angle brackets. In the Morse trainer, <AR>, <BT>, <SK> and <KN> come as a bonus after the 40 characters.

Common Q codes

CodeMeaning
QTHLocation (“my QTH is Boston”)
QRL?Is the frequency in use?
QRMInterference from other stations
QRNAtmospheric noise (static, thunderstorms)
QSBFading – the signal gets louder and quieter
QRSPlease send slower
QRZ?Who is calling me?
QSLReceipt confirmed
QSOA radio contact
QRTI am closing down

Abbreviations on the air

AbbreviationMeaning
CQGeneral call to everyone
DE“from” (CQ DE W1AW = “call from W1AW”)
RSTReport: readability, strength, tone (e.g. 599)
UR / ES / PSEyour / and / please
TNX / TUthanks / thank you
HW?How do you copy?
73Best regards
88Love and kisses

A typical short contact

CQ CQ DE W1AW K – W1AW DE G4XYZ <KN> – G4XYZ DE W1AW TNX UR RST 579 <BT> NAME TOM <BT> HW? <KN> – … – 73 <SK>

Frequently asked questions

What is a prosign?

Two or more letters sent without a gap as one sign. <AR> sounds like the single sign ,·–·–· not like A and R.

How are prosigns written?

With a bar over the letters or in angle brackets: <AR>, <SK>, <KN>. Our audio decoder shows them in angle brackets.

What does 73 mean?

“Best regards” – the friendly sign-off on the air. 88 means “love and kisses”.

What is the difference between K and KN?

K means “go ahead”, any station may answer. <KN> means “go ahead – only the station I am working”.

Do I need Morse code for an amateur radio licence?

In the US and most countries, no – the Morse requirement was dropped in the 2000s (the US in 2007). Q codes and common abbreviations are still part of the exams.
